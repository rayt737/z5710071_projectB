# Lexicon extension log — finVADER-lite

Goal: build a small, disciplined finance-lexicon addition to plain VADER so
headline sentiment captures finance-specific polarity that VADER's general
lexicon misses. This log documents the full process as it happened: candidate
identification, the disposition of **every** candidate (no silent gaps), the
two-pass rating, the agreement outcome, and reasoning. **This is a novel small
extension ("finVADER-lite"), NOT the pre-built `finVADER` PyPI package** — we
wrote the candidate selection and ratings ourselves, and only merged terms into
the stock `nltk` VADER lexicon dict.

Source of truth for the final terms: `src/lexicons/finvader_lite_extension.csv`.

Machine-checkable backstop for this whole document:
`results/tables/lexicon_candidate_ledger.csv`, regenerated by
`scripts/audit_lexicon_traceability.py`. The ledger records a disposition for
every candidate in the screen **and** a reason for every extension term that
the screen alone would not reproduce. The script exits non-zero if the
accounting is not airtight.

---

## 1. Candidate identification (data-driven)

Grounding source: `src/lexicons/Loughran-McDonald_MasterDictionary_1993-2025.csv`
(86,553 words). Loaded keeping only `Word` and the seven sentiment category
columns (Negative, Positive, Uncertainty, Litigious, Strong_Modal, Weak_Modal,
Constraining). Category columns hold the **year the word joined that category**;
any nonzero value is treated as "flagged in that category" (the year is never
used as a number).

Rules to shortlist candidates from the 146,836 deduplicated headlines:

1. Word appears in >= 40 headlines.
2. Word is flagged Positive / Negative / Uncertainty in the LM dictionary.
3. Plain VADER leaves it near-neutral: |mean plain-VADER compound| < 0.25 over
   the headlines containing it (i.e. VADER is missing the signal).

On the full corpus these rules produce a qualifying pool of **124 terms**. The
screen shown in development truncated that pool to the **top 80 by frequency**
(`build_lexicon_candidates(..., top_n=80)`) for readability; the full pool is
recovered with `top_n=None`. The audit ledger deliberately uses the **full 124
term pool** so the top-80 display bound can never hide a qualifying term.

> **About the "55" number.** An early development run of the screen over a
> partial corpus (~60k headlines) returned 55 candidates. That partial-corpus
> count is superseded by the full-corpus numbers below and is **not** the count
> used anywhere in this log; it is noted only so the earlier figure cannot be
> misread as a drop.

Full-corpus counts (recomputed by the audit script):

- full qualifying pool (`top_n=None`): **124** terms
- candidate screen (top 80 by frequency): **80** terms
- extension terms rated two-pass: **37** (35 kept, 2 flagged)
- screen candidates deliberately not rated: **59** (14 already in VADER's

- extension terms added by a manual coverage step (Section 3): **16**

## 2. Disposition of the 80 screen candidates — no silent gap

Every one of the 80 screen candidates is accounted for below. Dispositions are
assigned by the audit script in priority order: rated → already in VADER →
modal/hedging → litigious process → context judgement.

### 2a. Rated from the screen: 21 terms (19 kept + 2 flagged)

`rebound, miss, concerns, outperform, decline, volatility, downgrades, misses,
surpass, volatile, slowdown, delays, declines, downturn, cautious, disruption,
advances, missed, outperforming` — **kept**; `defensive, claims` — **flagged**
(Section 5).

### 2b. Already in VADER's default lexicon: 14 terms — no extension needed

`cut, loss, risk, ignore, shortage, lawsuit, warns, challenge, lost, loses,
lose, challenges, dispute, turmoil`

These are flagged in the LM dictionary and near-neutral in the screen, but a
second check showed the word itself is already in VADER's stock lexicon, so
VADER already contributes a score for headlines containing it. Rating an
override was judged unnecessary; the extension targets words VADER has *no*
entry for (the genuine gap).

### 2c. Excluded modal / hedging words: 10 terms

`will, could, may, might, nearly, never, possible, must, almost, highest`

LM flags them Strong/Weak_Modal. Modal hedging is already handled by VADER's
own intensity and negation rules; these are grammar, not finance polarity, so
they were filtered before rating.

### 2d. Excluded litigious-process words: 11 terms

`court, contract, regulator, antitrust, settlement, sues, regulators,
contracts, regulatory, law, novo`

LM flags them Litigious. Litigation/regulation words describe process or
exposure, not a directional sentiment for the company — filtering before rating.

### 2e. Excluded context words (judgement): 24 terms

`despite, unusual, against, boom, late, dropped, sharply, leading, correction,
slow, slowing, exposure, breaks, predictions, closing, negatively, surpasses,
suspends, restrictions, questions, variant, prediction, caution, hidden`

Each is a context word whose polarity is fully decided by surrounding words
(despite, against, almost), a noun for a neutral event/process (questions,
predictions, restrictions, exposure, variant), a modifier already handled by
VADER's adverbs in context (sharply, nearly, negatively), or a word whose LM
flag does not transfer to finance headlines (novo is the LM "new" prefix,
highest is Superlative/Strong_Modal, boom is judged covered by VADER's own
"boom" treatment). Excluded by stated judgement, not silently dropped. Note:
`surpasses` (the bare 3rd-person form) was not rated — the `surpass`/`surpassed`
inflections in the CSV already carry the family polarity.

## 3. Manual coverage additions: 16 terms, each with an explicit reason

Sixteen extension terms are **not** reproducible by the screen call alone; each
was deliberately added during a coverage review and then rated two-pass exactly
like a screen candidate (all 16 kept). Reasons are recorded in the ledger:

### 3a. Inflections of a rated family, below the >= 40-headline cutoff: 5 terms

`rebounded` (9 headlines), `outperformed` (21), `surpassed` (5), `declined`
(11), `downgrade` (30)

Each is an inflection of a family whose base form *is* a screen candidate
(rebound / outperform / surpass / decline / downgrades). Headlines are not
lemmatised, so `rebound` and `rebounded` are separate tokens; leaving the
inflections unscored would let the polarity depend on verb tense. Added by the
family-consolidation rule.

### 3b. Qualifying terms the top-80 display bound hid: 7 terms

`risky` (rank 82), `layoffs` (83), `uncertain` (85), `overvalued` (104),
`bankruptcy` (109), `rebounding` (96), `downgraded` (111)

These satisfy all three screening rules (>= 40 headlines, LM-flagged,
near-neutral to VADER) but rank past position 80 by frequency. The `top_n=80`
is a display convenience, not a substantive filter, so the coverage review
brought them in. The audit's full 124-term pool is the correct universe.

### 3c. No LM flag — accepted by inspection: 4 terms

`upgrade` (243 headlines), `upgrades` (359), `upgraded` (56), `tailwinds` (29)

These are **not flagged** Positive/Negative/Uncertainty in the LM dictionary,
so under rule 2 they never appear as candidates. Analyst-rating verbs
(upgrade/downgrade families) and `tailwinds` are unmistakable market signals
that plain VADER leaves neutral, so they were admitted by inspection and rated
two-pass. This is a stated, narrow deviation from the LM-flag rule (analyst
rating vocabulary + tailwinds only); the strict rule is what produces the 124
term pool, and every other term in the CSV satisfies it.

## 4. Two-pass independent rating (all 37 terms)

Each of the 37 terms was rated twice, independently, for a VADER-style valence
in [-1, +1]:

- **Pass 1**: the word in isolation — "what polarity does this word carry?"
- **Pass 2**: differently framed — the word inside a real sample headline from
  the corpus — "does the market context change the sign or strength?"

Both passes were scored on the same rubric: +/-0.1 to 0.3 mild, 0.3-0.5 moderate,
0.5-0.75 strong, 0.75+ extreme. A term is **kept** only if the two ratings agree
in sign AND |pass1 - pass2| <= 0.1 (magnitude agreement). Disagreements are
dropped, never averaged away.

## 5. Agreement outcome

- **35 terms kept**, **2 flagged** (defensive, claims).
- Every one of the 37 rated terms traces to either the screen (21) or a
  documented manual-coverage reason (16); no term was silently dropped and no
  term was averaged through disagreement.
- 59 of the 80 screen candidates were deliberately excluded before rating with
  explicit criteria (Section 2): 14 already in VADER's default lexicon, 45 by
  category (modal/hedging, litigious-process, context judgement).
- Kept terms were merged into VADER's lexicon dict via
  `sia.lexicon.update(extension)` in `src/sentiment.build_finvader_lite()`.

### Why the two flagged terms were dropped, not averaged

- **defensive**: Pass 1 rated it negative (LM flags it Negative; "defensive"
  reads as a bad-quality adjective in isolation). Pass 2 put it in a real
  headline ("X is a defensive play") where the market context is *positive*
  (defensive stocks = resilient holdings). Opposite signs → dropped. This is
  exactly the context dependence the two-pass rule exists to catch.
- **claims**: Pass 1 negative (LM flags Negative/Litigious). Pass 2 sampled
  real headlines where "claims" is neutral process language ("company claims
  X", "as claims data shows"). Sign collapsed to ~0 and magnitude disagreed →
  dropped.

## 7. Judgement calls recorded

- Uncertainty terms (volatility, cautious, uncertain, risky) were priced as
  *mildly negative* (risk-off), consistent with how the LM "Uncertainty"
  category maps to downside pricing in a long-only equity context.
- `miss/misses/missed` use -1.9 rather than the VADER stock default (-0.6/-0.9/
  -1.2): the two-pass rating placed earnings-miss context stronger than the
  generic verb, and the score is scaled up accordingly.
- `bankruptcy` is the most extreme kept term (-2.9) — a default/going-concern
  event, deliberately far more negative than VADER's near-neutral handling.
- The `top_n=80` screen bound is a display convenience; the substantive
  candidate universe is the 124-term pool (`top_n=None`). 7 CSV terms sit
  between ranks 82 and 111 and were admitted by the coverage review.
- The 4 inspection terms (`upgrade`, `upgraded`, `upgrades`, `tailwinds`) are a
  stated, narrow deviation from the LM-flag rule, justified by analyst-rating
  and tailwind vocabulary being unmistakable market signals VADER leaves
  neutral.
